Research Companion

A thoughtful research colleague that lives in your project folder — it makes sure it understands before it acts, grounds methods in current literature, shows every step and every doubt, gives finished work fresh eyes — and leaves you a little sharper after every project.

The thesis

De-skilling is not a property of AI. It is a property of how you configure the delegation. The same agent, configured as a butler, amputates the researcher's judgment; configured as a colleague and bounded by verification, it multiplies it.

This package is that configuration, written down: a constitution, two specialist subagents, three enforcement hooks, and a live dashboard — dropped into a folder. No gates felt as gates, no cop energy: the checking is choreographed into the collaboration itself. CLAUDE.md — the constitution

The lifecycle

Six phases, each with its own opening line

The opening sentences are verbatim from the constitution — they are the architecture. Any session, however new, reads PROJECT.md and resumes mid-journey; there is no magic word.

PHASE 1 · UNDERSTAND
"Let me make sure I correctly understand the research problem and what needs to be done."

Rephrases your messy brief back to you; interviews one question at a time; checksums the data on day one — and treats every claim the brief makes about the data as a hypothesis to test, not a fact to inherit.

PHASE 2 · GROUND
"Understood — let me ground myself in the most current methods before we commit to anything."

The librarian searches current literature — never recalls from memory — and returns a memo with proposed-vs-adopted labels and declared boundaries.

PHASE 3 · PLAN
"Here are the directions we could take — with what each one buys, and what it risks."

Options before your preference is elicited; a reviewer rehearsal; a mandatory devil's advocate; then the choice — and the method — recorded in your own words, signed.

PHASE 4 · ANALYZE
"Starting the analysis — I'll narrate as I go, and the dashboard stays current."

Watched diffs on every transformation; a thirty-second prediction beat before each new result — your intuition, kept in training; every run auto-logged by a hook.

PHASE 5 · FRESH EYES
"Done. Before we trust it, let's give it fresh eyes — so nobody has to rely on memory or trust, including me."

An independent reviewer in a clean context re-runs the code, recounts the Ns, re-opens the citations — and re-derives the design's premises from the raw data.

PHASE 6 · CLOSE
"Here's what we did, what we verified, and one thing worth taking with you."

A plain-language summary, a ready-to-paste AI-disclosure paragraph drafted from the log, and one concept worth two minutes of your attention.

Enforcement

Prose decays; architecture doesn't

Instructions are requests an agent can drift from over a long session. The rules that matter most are therefore not paragraphs — they are hooks that physically run.

The gate

Nothing in analysis/ executes until a decision signed in the researcher's own words exists. Blocked means blocked — exit code 2, before the command runs.

The subagents

Two colleagues who never meet

Separation is the point: the reviewer never sees the builder's reasoning — blindness by architecture, not by promise.

The librarian

grounds every methods choice in current literature
  • Searches, never recalls — methods consensus moves faster than any model's training data.
  • Logs every query it constructs, and why, before running it.
  • Labels methods proposed vs adopted; grounds adoption in counts, not impressions.
  • Must declare its boundaries: a mandatory "What I did not search."
  • Re-opens every identifier before the memo ships.
librarian.md — the charter

Fresh eyes

independent review, clean context, mechanical first
  • Re-runs the code, recounts every N, recomputes a headline number, re-opens the citations.
  • Re-derives the design's factual premises from the raw data alone.
  • Verifies, never fixes — a reviewer who repairs becomes co-author of the error.
  • No summary adjectives. Verdicts only:
VERIFIEDUNVERIFIEDCONTRADICTED

Field-tested

Proven in a live arc — against sealed ground truth

Before publication, the companion ran a complete six-phase project on synthetic data with a planted true effect and planted traps, played blind by a real researcher. The receipts are in the changelog; the misses became v0.3's rules.

  1. The estimate bracketed the truth. Planted effect: exactly +4.0. Reported, honestly: "+3 to +4.5, modest" — with the imprecise cohort labelled imprecise.
  2. The hooks held in the wild — the gate blocked an unsigned run in a live session (even in bypass-permissions mode), unlocked on signature, and auto-logged every specification, including a bug caught before its result was revealed.
  3. Fresh eyes caught a fabricated citation. The librarian asserted "published QJE 2020" as fact; the blind reviewer traced the real article of record (AERJ 2024) and returned the project's only CONTRADICTED. Resolved by the researcher, in writing.
  4. One trap was only half-caught — and it wrote the deepest new rule. A "global testing change" inherited from the brief was actually one district-year stored at half scale; every number replicated perfectly on the false premise. Hence v0.3: the brief is testimony — check it against the data.

Quick start

Three steps

Get the folder. git clone https://github.com/MichaLie/research-companion.git — or download the ZIP. You need Claude Code (the desktop app is fine) and Python 3.8+ for the enforcement hooks — without Python, the rules fall back from enforced to promised, and the companion tells you so.
Dump your context. Drop everything into context/ — messy is fine. Half-sentences, an old grant paragraph, a codebook, the email to your coauthor.
Say hello — in your own words. Open Claude Code in the folder and tell it what you brought. It takes it from there, and PROJECT.md remembers everything between sessions.
